# AI Image Generation and Reference-Editing Quality Checklist

This checklist is for creators who need repeatable results from text-to-image generation or reference-image editing. It focuses on parts of a brief that can be evaluated objectively, so a prompt can be improved without relying on vague reactions such as “make it better.”

## 1. Define the job before the prompt

Record the intended use before describing the image:

- **Output type:** product visual, social post, thumbnail, concept frame, portrait, or template asset
- **Viewer:** customer, follower, client, internal reviewer, or general audience
- **Format:** square, portrait, landscape, transparent background, or a specific aspect ratio
- **Primary subject:** the one element that must remain visually dominant
- **Success condition:** what a reviewer should be able to recognize or understand immediately

A prompt is easier to evaluate when the intended output is explicit. “A premium product hero image for a mobile landing page” provides more direction than “make a beautiful picture.”

## 2. Build prompts in layers

A practical prompt can be assembled in this order:

1. **Subject** — what must appear
2. **Action or state** — what the subject is doing
3. **Environment** — where the scene takes place
4. **Composition** — framing, viewpoint, spacing, and subject placement
5. **Lighting** — soft daylight, studio key light, rim light, overcast, or another specific setup
6. **Material and detail** — surfaces, textures, fabric, reflections, and small features
7. **Visual direction** — editorial, documentary, minimal, cinematic, playful, or technical
8. **Output constraints** — aspect ratio, background, text-free area, or required consistency

Example structure:

> A matte ceramic skincare bottle on pale stone, centered three-quarter view, soft window light from the left, subtle shadow, restrained neutral palette, premium editorial product photography, clean negative space above the subject, vertical composition.

## 3. Reference-image editing checklist

When a reference image is used, separate what must stay fixed from what may change.

### Preserve

- identity or recognizable subject traits
- product shape, logo placement, and proportions
- camera angle when consistency matters
- important colors and materials
- hand, face, or object count

### Change

- background or location
- lighting direction and time of day
- wardrobe or surface styling
- color grade
- crop, framing, or aspect ratio
- nonessential props

State preservation requirements first. If an edit changes too many variables at once, make one controlled change per iteration so the cause of improvement or failure remains visible.

## 4. Review the generated image

Use the same review order for every candidate:

- **Brief match:** Does the image solve the intended job?
- **Subject accuracy:** Are shape, identity, count, and proportions correct?
- **Composition:** Is the focal point obvious? Is required negative space available?
- **Lighting:** Do highlights and shadows agree with the described light source?
- **Edges and anatomy:** Check fingers, hair, jewelry, text-like marks, product edges, and repeated objects.
- **Material realism:** Do glass, metal, fabric, skin, food, or plastic behave consistently?
- **Brand safety:** Is unintended text, a conflicting logo, or an unsuitable visual association present?
- **Delivery readiness:** Is the aspect ratio, crop, and resolution appropriate for the destination?

## 5. Keep a small iteration log

For each useful result, save:

- the exact prompt
- the model or generation mode
- reference images used
- aspect ratio and settings
- what was changed from the previous attempt
- a short reason the result passed or failed

This turns prompting into a reproducible workflow instead of a sequence of guesses.
